# Campus Pilot Access and Module Model

This is the canonical product and engineering reference for sign-in, roles, permissions, modules, and licensing.

## 1. Sign-in and landing

- Everyone uses the same sign-in screen.
- A successful sign-in opens the campus module launcher at `/home`; it never opens Administration directly.
- The launcher shows only modules that are both enabled for the campus and available to the signed-in user.
- Administration is a module, not the application shell. Its navigation contains administration concerns only.
- A future platform operator console is separate from campus Administration. Platform operators do not inherit access to school operational records.

## 2. Roles and permissions

- A user may have multiple roles.
- A role has an immutable, tenant-scoped `key` used by assignments and an editable `name` shown to people.
- Seeded roles and the core Home and Administration module records are provisioned atomically when a campus tenant is created. A seeded role keeps its code-owned permission baseline and cannot be deleted; its presentation name and description may be changed. Schools create custom roles when responsibilities differ from a seeded baseline.
- Custom roles may be created and deleted dynamically.
- `roles:create`, `roles:edit`, and `roles:assign` are explicit access-administration authorities. A School Administrator may create ordinary catalog-based roles and assign any non-wildcard seeded or custom role even when that role grants operational permissions the administrator does not personally use.
- Only a Campus Owner may create, edit, or assign a role containing `*`. The Campus Owner account cannot be edited, deactivated, or deleted from the user directory, and no operator may manage their own account there.
- An assigned custom role must be removed from every user before deletion. Role names are unique per campus without regard to case, while immutable role keys preserve assignments across renames.
- User and role drawers mirror these rules by hiding unavailable actions, but every mutation is authorized again by the API. Deactivation and deletion revoke refresh sessions; access tokens are rejected immediately because every request reloads the active user record.
- Module navigation is persona-aware. Academic setup areas such as years, terms, grade levels, subjects, teacher profiles, classes, teaching assignments, and assessment structures are shown only to academic administrators. Teachers enter assigned marks and comments through Gradebook and Progress and reporting; the seeded Teacher role never receives academic setup permissions.
- Authorization uses stable permission keys controlled by the application, never labels, URL paths, or substring matching.
- A permission is `<namespace>:<action>`, for example `users:view`, `fleet:create`, or `timetabling:manage`.
- `*` grants all permissions inside modules enabled for the campus. It does not bypass licensing.
- Fine-grained record scope such as `self`, `assigned classes`, or `department` belongs in policy/data-scope rules; it must not be simulated by renaming CRUD permissions.

The initial seeded campus roles are:

- Campus Owner — full access to every enabled campus module.
- School Administrator — manages people, access, configuration, and licensing without becoming a platform operator.
- Teacher — assigned-class teaching, learner, timetable, library, and communication access; no academic-structure or teacher-profile administration.
- Student — self-service learning, timetable, fee, library, and communication access.
- Registrar — admissions, enrolment, and learner-record access.
- Academic Manager — campus-wide assessment setup, mark-sheet publication, and controlled corrections.
- Finance Officer — finance, fees, procurement, and reporting access.
- Librarian — library operations and learner lookup.
- Hostel Officer — residences, rooms, learner allocations, and campus pastoral records.
- Health Officer — campus health records, clinic visits, medication, and follow-up.
- Internal Auditor — campus audit-plan visibility plus assigned engagement fieldwork and draft findings.
- Audit Manager — campus-wide audit planning, engagement oversight, finding issue authority, and numbering policy.
- Staff Member — employee self-service, timetable, communication, and library access.

## 3. Canonical people and cross-module profiles

Campus Pilot separates a person's campus record from their ability to sign in.

- A `user` is an optional system account used for authentication, roles, permissions, Sessions, and audit attribution. It is not the canonical school-domain record for an employee, learner, guardian, or supplier contact.
- An `employee` is the canonical workforce record owned by HR and payroll. It may link to at most one user account in the same campus, and an account may link to at most one active employee. Creating an employee never silently creates login credentials.
- Dated employment engagements are the canonical contract and assignment history. At most one engagement is active for an employee; activating or ending it transactionally updates the employee directory's current department, position, dates, and status projection. Ended and cancelled decisions cannot be reopened or silently rewritten.
- HR availability periods have a forward-only draft, submission, decision, and cancellation lifecycle. Approved periods are serialized per employee, cannot overlap, and expose a minimum-field typed scheduling reference to Timetabling, Fleet, and other authorised modules. Medical/personal notes remain inside HR and are not part of that cross-module reference.
- Fleet does not own a second driver person record. A driver is a one-to-one Fleet profile attached to an active employee and contains only driver-specific facts such as licence number, class, expiry, and driver status. Names, work contact details, department, position, and login identity are read from the employee.
- Fleet exposes a minimum-field employee-candidate read for driver assignment without granting HR management permissions. Because Fleet driver profiles depend on the employee directory, Fleet entitlements include HR and payroll as a module dependency.
- A teacher is an employee with teaching assignments and any teacher-specific profile owned by Academics. A staff role grants access; it does not create or replace the employee record.
- Academics owns academic years, terms, canonical tenant-wide grade levels, subjects, classes, employee-backed teacher profiles, teaching assignments, and term-scoped assessment structures. Creating or changing those records is academic administration and uses `academics:create`, `academics:edit`, `academics:delete`, or `academics:manage`; the seeded Academic Manager carries that authority. The seeded Teacher does not. Teachers receive `academics:teach` only for assigned Gradebook draft creation, mark capture, submission, and assigned report comments, with assigned record scopes enforced independently. A class belongs to an academic year and references a canonical grade level by identifier; the display label is never copied into the class record. Academic terms are contained by their year, cannot overlap, move only forward through planned, active, and closed states, and keep their year/code/date boundary after activation. Assessment cycles move forward from draft to open to closed; their components reference canonical teaching assignments and must total exactly 100% per assignment before opening. Class, subject, teacher, and year labels are resolved from the assignment rather than copied into assessment records. SIS owns learner enrolment and class placement, so mark capture validates learner eligibility through SIS instead of creating an Academics learner table. Academics entitlements include HR and payroll when teacher identity is resolved from the employee directory.
- Timetabling requires an active Academics term, consumes canonical academic records, and reads approved HR availability through the minimum-field scheduling projection. Dated HR availability remains a planning constraint rather than a permanent weekly rule. Timetabling owns recurring scheduling rules, rooms, immutable generated snapshots, and publication state. Timetabling operations declare both Academics and HR and payroll as direct module dependencies.
- A learner is the canonical person/enrolment record in People and admissions. Ordinary learner creation transactionally assigns an immutable number from the tenant-owned prefix, padding, and monotonic sequence policy (default `LRN-000001`); staged imports preserve explicit legacy learner numbers and align values in the managed namespace before commit. The policy read requires `sis:view`; a change requires `sis:edit`, the loaded optimistic version, a forward-only next sequence, and an audit reason. Both operations require only the SIS entitlement, not Academics. Student login is an optional linked account. Guardian relationships attach to the learner/person records and may independently link to user accounts.
- Applications reference an Academics-owned academic year and canonical target grade by stable campus-scoped identifiers. Class placement belongs to enrolment; when an accepted application is selected, the enrolment class must belong to the requested grade. SIS does not copy academic records, and an enrolment cannot be moved to another learner after creation.
- SIS data imports are licensed SIS operations. CSV/XLSX source bytes and unmapped source columns are retained privately with a fingerprint, never returned by read APIs or Agent capabilities. Every mapping creates a new immutable version and normalized preview, and only an explicit idempotent commit creates ready learner or guardian rows. Invalid and duplicate rows retain results; imports never create user accounts. The shared parser is destination-neutral and is now used by SIS, HR, and Fees while each destination keeps its own canonical validation and commit authority.
- Attendance owns dated class registers, per-learner marks, learner history, and their draft/submitted lifecycle. It requires licensed Academics and SIS modules, resolves the active term, classes, and current teaching assignments through typed Academics operations, and builds each roster from date-effective SIS enrolments. Teachers receive `attendance:view`, `attendance:create`, `attendance:edit`, and `attendance:submit` with `attendance.registers=assigned`; they can work only in classes they currently teach. Attendance Officers receive campus scope and retain `attendance:manage` and `attendance:delete` for corrections and draft cleanup. It stores stable learner and enrolment identifiers rather than copied names or numbers. Draft registers use optimistic versions; submission requires every learner to be marked, locks the roster, and appends an immutable full-roster mark snapshot. A manager must give a retained reason to reopen it. Register lifecycle and submitted-mark evidence are append-only, and learner history returns only the current accepted submitted version.
- E-learning owns class-linked learning spaces, ordered units, governed resource links, and their publication history. It requires licensed Academics, SIS, HR and payroll, and Document Registry modules. A space references one current teaching assignment, academic year, term, and class by stable identifier; it never copies a second roster, teacher, subject, or file store. Teachers receive `learning:view` and `learning:teach` with assigned scope, students receive `learning:view` with self scope through current enrolment, Academic Managers receive `learning:view`, `learning:teach`, and `learning:manage` with campus scope, and Campus Owners retain wildcard access. Content is authored as drafts in the order resource, unit, then space; published records are immutable, while withdrawal and archive are explicit reasoned lifecycle transitions. Learners see only published spaces, units, and resources for their current class. Direct uploads are filed through the configured Document Registry classification; linking or upload failure never deletes an already governed official file.
- Student Support owns restricted learner-support cases, assigned case teams, append-only actions, and reasoned escalation, resolution, and closure evidence. SIS remains authoritative for learner identity; cases store only the learner identifier and resolve the current name and learner number through typed SIS operations. The seeded Student Support Case Worker receives `student_support:view`, `student_support:create`, and `student_support:edit` with `student_support.cases=assigned`, may open a case only with themselves as lead, and loses access immediately when removed from the active team. The seeded Student Support Manager receives campus scope plus `student_support:manage` for team and lifecycle decisions. Teacher, School Administrator, Registrar, Student, and Guardian receive no Student Support permission or record scope by default. Every case always has a non-null lead, uses optimistic versions for writes, has no hard-delete route, and retains append-only action and lifecycle evidence with actor-aware audit in the same transaction.
- Gradebook is an Academics workspace with a separate `cp-gradebook` domain boundary. It requires licensed Academics, SIS, and HR and payroll modules, resolves each assessment component through Academics, and snapshots date-effective SIS enrolments by stable identifiers. Marks are stored as exact integer hundredths. Teachers use `academics:teach` to create, capture, and submit drafts only for assigned components; `academics:manage` holders publish or reopen submitted and published sheets with a retained reason. Published sheets are required before an assessment cycle can close, and lifecycle events are append-only.
- Progress & reporting is an Academics workspace with a separate `cp-academic-reporting` boundary. It requires licensed Attendance, SIS, and HR and payroll dependencies and consumes published Gradebook marks, submitted Attendance summaries, and the SIS roster through typed owning-module operations. Academic managers configure grading schemes, generate report batches, review progression, and publish. Teachers may see and comment on assigned-class drafts. Accounts granted self scope, including linked student or guardian accounts, see only published learner records linked to that account. Report-time learner and subject labels, calculated subject results, and attendance totals are immutable snapshots; remarks and progression decisions use optimistic versions; reviewed or published batches must be reopened with a retained reason before changes.
- Communication owns announcement drafts, reviewed audience targets, frozen recipient snapshots, in-app delivery state, and personal inbox read state. It never owns a second contact directory: campus and role audiences resolve active core accounts, class audiences resolve Academics classes plus SIS learner and opted-in guardian account links, department audiences resolve HR employees, and individual audiences resolve an existing active user. Teachers receive assigned scope and may maintain only their own drafts for assigned classes; Communication Managers receive campus scope and separate publication authority; learner, guardian, registrar, and staff roles receive self scope. Submission freezes the exact deduplicated active-account set and its fingerprint; publication delivers that snapshot without re-resolving changed rosters. Published communication is immutable and may only be cancelled with a retained reason. In-app is the only implemented delivery channel; email and SMS remain provider-neutral adapters and must never be reported as sent before an adapter produces delivery evidence.
- Finance owns currencies, accounts, accounting periods, and journals. Journals keep typed source-record references so Fees, Procurement, Fleet, Assets and inventory, and other modules can trace their Finance effect without duplicating ledger rows. Transaction and reporting values use integer minor units, foreign-currency lines retain the applied rate, drafts must balance, and only an open period may accept a new posting. The preparer cannot approve or post the same journal; posted entries are immutable and are corrected through a separately reviewed reversal. CSV/XLSX workflows stop at a normalized preview and explicit draft creation; an import never posts a journal implicitly.
- Fees owns one immutable-identity billing account per SIS learner, versioned fee structures scoped to Academics-owned years, terms, and grades, and immutable issued invoices. Fee values use the selected Finance currency's integer minor units; structures reference active Finance posting asset and income accounts through typed owning-module operations. Draft structures may be edited or removed, active structures may only be retired, and closed billing accounts cannot be reopened. Billing-account and invoice reads are campus-wide only for Fees administrators; other `fees:view` users are restricted to learner records linked to their current login. Fees operations declare SIS, Academics, and Finance as licensed dependencies. Issuing an invoice creates one idempotent, balanced Finance posting request; it does not create or post a journal. Finance alone may convert that request into a journal draft, after which the existing submit, independent approval, and posting controls apply. Staged billing-account imports require `fees:create`, resolve existing SIS learner numbers, preserve an optional legacy account number, deduplicate by learner and account number, retain immutable previews, and create only explicitly committed ready rows. They never create logins, invoices, Finance posting requests, or journal entries. Receipts and adjustments follow the same source-record boundary instead of writing ledger rows.
- Library owns catalogue titles, physical-copy state, memberships, loans, holds, fines, and append-only activity evidence. It does not own a second person directory: each membership references exactly one SIS learner or HR employee and resolves the current name, number, source status, and optional login link through typed owning-module reads. Physical copies receive immutable accession numbers from a tenant-configurable, transaction-locked prefix, padding, and monotonic sequence; a retired or lost number is never reused. Learners, teachers, and staff receive self scope for memberships and borrowing records, while Librarians receive campus scope and separate catalogue, circulation, and management permissions. Lending days, loan limits, renewal limits, and overdue-fine policy are tenant settings. Monetary values use an active Finance currency and integer minor units. A learner fine may submit one idempotent typed charge request to the matching active Fees billing account; Library never writes an invoice, balance, receipt, payment, posting request, or journal, and employee fines remain Library-owned until a suitable employee-receivable boundary exists.
- Hostel owns residences, rooms, learner allocation history, pastoral records, and append-only activity evidence. It references SIS learners by stable identifier and resolves current learner names and numbers through typed SIS operations instead of creating another person directory. A learner may have only one planned or active allocation. New allocations and active transfers require a server preview fingerprint derived from the requested dates, current room version, and overlapping allocation evidence; the transaction locks and rechecks that evidence before committing. Planned allocations may become active or cancelled, active allocations may end or transfer, and completed decisions are retained rather than rewritten. Hostel Officers receive campus scope for `hostel.occupancy` and `hostel.pastoral`; dynamic roles may receive campus or self occupancy scope, while pastoral reads remain campus-scoped and sensitive. Hostel operations require the licensed SIS dependency.
- Document Registry owns tenant-scoped official-file references, classification snapshots, retention dates, private object locations, content hashes, and append-only lifecycle evidence. It accepts only bounded PDF, JPEG, and PNG files whose declared media type matches their signature, requires a clean internal malware scan before private object persistence, and never exposes its bucket through the generic public storage route. Records Officers receive campus scope for `document_registry.records`; `document_registry:restricted` is an additional gate for restricted metadata and human downloads. A file moves from filed to closed to destroyed and is never reopened. Due disposition requires a reasoned request and an independent approval; an approved destruction then requires explicit execution. Destruction removes the private bytes but retains the reference, hash, classification snapshot, decision, and audit evidence. Classifications are campus policy, so no legal retention series are invented during setup.
- Internal Audit owns campus numbering policies, audit plans, assigned engagements, immutable evidence-link snapshots, findings, lifecycle transitions, and append-only audit events. It never edits the source transaction being audited and never stores Document Registry bytes, object keys, hashes, download links, or private actor identifiers in its evidence projection. Linking evidence requires the licensed Document Registry dependency and snapshots only the authorised document identifier, reference, title, sensitivity, and stated audit purpose. Internal Auditors receive campus plan scope and assigned engagement scope; they may lead only their own assigned work and draft findings. Audit Managers receive campus plan and engagement scope plus separate delete, issue, and manage authority. Restricted documents still require the independent `document_registry:restricted` permission. A plan moves from draft to approved to closed, an engagement moves from planned to fieldwork to reporting to closed, and a finding moves from draft to issued; completed states are retained rather than rewritten.
- Health services owns patient enrolment into care, care alerts, clinic visits, medication authorization and administration history, follow-ups, and append-only activity evidence. A Health patient references exactly one SIS learner or HR employee; names, numbers, guardian contacts, account links, and employment details remain in their source modules and are resolved through typed minimum-field reads. Medication administration history is append-only. Health Officers receive campus scope for `health.patients` and `health.care`; ordinary campus, teaching, learner, and staff roles do not receive Health access merely because they can see the same person elsewhere. Dynamic roles may receive explicit self or campus Health record scope, but every API and Agent read re-evaluates that scope. Health services operations require licensed SIS and HR and payroll dependencies.
- Procurement owns suppliers, requisitions, purchase orders, and goods receipts, while requester identity remains HR-owned and currency remains Finance-owned. A purchase order may be drafted only from an approved requisition and an active supplier; it snapshots the requisition, requester, supplier, currency, and selected source lines, and its issued quantities may not exceed the approved requisition. Draft details freeze on issue, and neither the creator nor the latest draft editor may issue the same order. Receiving requires separate `procurement:receive` authority and records draft goods receipts only against open issued orders. Neither the creator nor the latest draft editor may post the receipt; posting makes it immutable, prevents cumulative receipts from exceeding ordered quantities, and transactionally advances the purchase order to partially or fully received. Direct purchase-order status changes cannot claim receipt progress without matching posted receipt lines. These Procurement records do not write Finance journals or posting requests.
- Assets and inventory owns the tenant item master, store directory, immutable stock-movement ledger, transactional stock-balance projection, and goods-receipt allocation mapping. The server assigns immutable, monotonic `ITM-000001`, `STR-000001`, and `MOV-000001` references; clients never supply or recycle them. Item quantities use an exact scaled-integer contract: `quantity_scale` is fixed when the item is created, thresholds and stock quantities use that scale, and floating-point quantities, implicit rescaling, and rounding are prohibited. Manual receipts, issues, transfers, adjustments, reversals, and explicit goods-receipt allocations append movements rather than editing or deleting ledger history; a reversal is an Assets-owned compensating movement linked to its source and requires only the Assets and inventory entitlement. Procurement owns the immutable posted goods-receipt source. Assets owns the explicit mapping and resulting receipt movement, so only the `GET` and `POST` goods-receipt-allocation operations declare Procurement as a direct dependency. No current Assets operation depends on Finance or HR and payroll; valuation and employee custodianship remain later, separately governed boundaries. A useful LADS concept for later is a school-specific department or cost-centre inventory request followed by approval and store issue, but it supplies no stock-ledger code, route, or authorization model to reuse.
- Every future module persona follows this same rule: authentication is a `user`, the school-domain person is owned once by HR or SIS, and module-specific responsibilities are profiles over that person. Roles control access; they do not manufacture duplicate people.
- Cross-module references use stable IDs and typed owning-module services. A consuming module must not copy a person's name, phone, email, department, or account state into its own profile table merely to display it.

This identity pattern keeps the useful LADS employee/account foundation while removing its automatic inactive-user creation and duplicated module-owned person fields.

## 4. Module registry

The application owns one module catalog. Each entry has a stable key, label, group, route, permission namespace, description, and availability state.

- Core modules (`home`, `administration`) are enabled during campus setup and do not require a commercial key.
- Licensed modules are enabled by an installation-bound signed entitlement lease scoped to a campus and explicit module keys.
- The API exchanges a one-time activation code, verifies the signed lease, stores its immutable claims and fingerprint plus a normalized current projection, and never persists the submitted activation code.
- Expired, revoked, or disabled modules disappear from the launcher and are rejected by the API even when a role contains matching permissions.
- Existing installations are grandfathered during the access-model migration so working modules are not silently removed before a renewable lease is connected. The first accepted signed lease becomes authoritative in the same transaction: granted legacy rows become lease-managed, omitted legacy rows are revoked, deliberate local-disable choices on still-granted modules survive, and core Home/Administration rows remain outside commercial takeover. New campuses start with core modules and require entitlements for licensed modules.

Initial school module catalog:

- Agent
- People and admissions
- Academics
- Attendance
- E-learning
- Student support
- Timetabling
- Communication
- Finance
- Fees and billing
- Library
- HR and payroll
- Procurement
- Fleet
- Hostel
- Health services
- Assets and inventory
- Document registry
- Internal audit

The final three adopt useful LADS concepts in school language. LADS route-string authorization and unfiltered module walls are deliberately not carried forward.

### Commercial licensing and control plane

Commercial licensing is split across two deployables:

- **Campus Pilot control plane** is a separate vendor-operated Cloudflare service. It owns customer accounts, plans, prices, subscriptions, payments, installations, entitlement issuance, renewal, revocation, signing-key rotation, and vendor audit. It never receives learner, guardian, staff, health, payroll, finance, or other school operational records.
- **Campus entitlement runtime** remains inside each Campus Pilot installation. It stores and verifies signed leases, materializes effective entitlements, applies local module enablement, evaluates dependencies and limits, and continues operating without a synchronous control-plane call.

The control plane provides two distinct authenticated workspaces:

- The customer portal lets an authorized campus customer compare plans, purchase or renew a subscription, manage billing, register installations, create one-time activation codes, and download an offline license bundle.
- The owner portal is for platform operators only. It shows customers, subscriptions, payment state, installations, issued leases, entitlement changes, revocations, signing-key state, and append-only operator audit. Platform access never implies access to a school's operational application or data.

Assigning a subscription does not itself connect a campus server. A customer administrator creates a short-lived, one-time activation code under Customer portal → Installations. A Campus Owner enters that code under Campus Pilot Administration → Licensing. The campus server exchanges it for a signed lease and encrypted renewable installation credential. The owner portal may inspect or revoke the resulting installation, but it does not impersonate the customer or issue the customer's activation code.

Online activation does not expose a reusable license key for the customer to store. The customer portal shows the activation code once, reports whether copying succeeded, and names the exact Campus Pilot screen where it must be used. After acceptance, the campus server owns renewal and keeps the installation credential encrypted.

Hosted payment checkout and billing management are used so Campus Pilot and the control plane do not collect or store card details. Payment-provider webhooks are signature-verified and idempotent. A client redirect or checkout success page is never authority to enable a module.

Billing is provider-neutral and multi-currency from the first contract. A plan may have several provider-specific price mappings in currencies such as ZWG, USD, or ZAR. Every money value uses an ISO 4217 currency code, an explicit currency exponent, and integer minor units; code never assumes two decimal places. Original, settlement, fee, and refund money remain distinct, and no report silently adds or converts unlike currencies. Stripe, PayPal, Paynow, Pesepay, and future providers integrate through isolated adapters rather than provider-named commercial columns.

### Signed entitlement lease v1

The canonical wire contract is versioned as `cp-license/v1` and carried in an Ed25519-signed JWS. The protected header includes `alg`, `typ`, and `kid`. The independent `catalog_version` identifies the code-owned module, feature, limit, and operation vocabulary; it is never derived from a commercial plan key. The current control plane issues `campus-pilot/3`. During the coordinated Learning rollout this runtime accepts both `campus-pilot/2` and `campus-pilot/3`; newly issued leases use only version 3. Claims include:

- issuer, audience, tenant ID, installation ID, lease ID, catalog version, and monotonic lease sequence;
- issue time, not-before time, refresh-after time, active lease deadline, grace deadline, and final token expiry;
- entitled module, feature, capability, and meter keys;
- limits with unit, period, value, and enforcement mode;
- optional minimum and maximum supported Campus Pilot versions.

The signing private key exists only in the control plane. Campus installations select an exact trusted public key by the signed `kid` header and reject unknown identifiers before signature verification. `LICENSE_PUBLIC_KEY_BASE64` plus `LICENSE_PUBLIC_KEY_ID` supplies the current key; `LICENSE_TRUSTED_PUBLIC_KEYS_JSON` supplies an optional identifier-to-base64 keyring for overlap. Rotation provisions the new public key to campuses before the control plane begins signing with it, keeps both old and new keys trusted through the maximum old lease/offline/grace lifetime, then removes the old key only after evidence shows it is no longer in use. The public `/api/v1/keys` response aids provisioning but is never fetched synchronously by an operational authorization decision. Activation credentials and installation renewal credentials are write-only secrets and are never logged, returned after creation, or stored in plaintext.

The local decision is an intersection, never a union:

```text
trusted installation and signed lease
AND lease state permits the requested operation
AND module or feature is commercially entitled
AND the module is locally enabled
AND declared dependencies are satisfied
AND the person's exact permission and record scope allow it
AND policy, approval, and quota checks allow it
```

The pure operation evaluator returns stable reason codes for module, feature, local enablement, dependency, lease lifecycle, application version, permission, record scope, quota, and approval decisions. Operation catalog version 4 assigns stable, exact descriptors to the 521 currently implemented authenticated campus operations. Every descriptor has a mandatory Agent exposure classification: 199 reads are exposed, 287 mutations require approval, 23 credential, private-byte, destructive, or restricted-reference operations remain human-only with explicit reasons, and 12 operations are prohibited. Student Support contributes 12 licensed operations: three highly sensitive scoped reads, eight approval-required mutations, and one human-only reference-data operation, all requiring SIS as a direct dependency. Learning contributes 20 licensed operations: five scoped metadata reads, 13 approval-required mutations, and two human-only private-file operations, all requiring Academics, SIS, HR and payroll, and Document Registry as direct dependencies. Internal Audit contributes 26 licensed operations: nine typed reads and 17 approval-required mutations, all requiring Document Registry as a direct dependency. Attendance contributes nine licensed operations: four typed reads and five approval-required mutations, all requiring Academics and SIS as direct dependencies. Gradebook contributes nine Academics operations: three reads and six approval-required mutations, all requiring SIS and HR and payroll as direct dependencies. Progress & reporting contributes 17 Academics operations: six reads and 11 approval-required mutations, all requiring Attendance, SIS, and HR and payroll as direct dependencies. Communication contributes 15 operations: seven scoped typed reads and eight approval-required mutations; audience construction depends on Academics, SIS, and HR and payroll, while inbox and stored delivery reads remain Communication-owned. This is coverage policy rather than execution authority: only a broker-backed typed capability may execute an exposed or approval-required operation. Actix route-pattern tests verify that every descriptor resolves to its intended route, and catalog tests reject duplicate keys, duplicate route identities, unknown modules, unknown permissions, and missing Agent classifications. Catalogued permission-authoritative routes enforce the complete evaluator decision at the API boundary instead of inferring access from a URL namespace and HTTP method. This includes lease lifecycle and module state, keeps cross-module dependency checks code-owned, keeps licensing repair under `licensing:edit`, and returns a generic denial without exposing commercial or authorization internals. Only launcher catalog and tenant-module discovery are authentication-only because every signed-in role needs them before entering a module. Licensing status and school settings are permission-authoritative. Agent discovery hides unlicensed capabilities, but every Agent execution evaluates licensing, permissions, scope, policy, approval, and quota again.

Each accepted signed lease is stored as immutable evidence and, in the same database transaction, replaces the tenant's normalized current entitlement projection. The projection records the source lease, catalog version, feature grants, capability limits, and minimum or maximum supported Campus Pilot versions. Runtime authorization computes application-version compatibility against the currently running binary instead of persisting a boolean that could become stale after an upgrade. Module dependency requirements remain versioned, code-owned operation-catalog rules and are evaluated against the projected module grants. Installations with a lease accepted before migration 009 temporarily read equivalent claims from that trusted lease until the next successful refresh creates the normalized projection. Grandfathered module rows remain usable only until the first signed lease; that lease atomically converts granted legacy rows to lease-managed rows and revokes any legacy module it does not grant.

Hard-enforced limits use UTC lifetime, day, month, or year buckets with transactional capacity reservations. The engine locks the current lease definition and period bucket, expires abandoned reservations, prevents concurrent overspend, resolves tenant-scoped idempotent retries to one reservation, and moves capacity from reserved to committed or released state. A commit writes one append-only usage event carrying the source lease, code-owned operation, meter key, unit, amount, period, and optional actor. Active exhausted keys are loaded into the trusted entitlement snapshot so the pure operation evaluator returns `quota_exceeded`. No released route currently declares a production meter and the live Complete plan currently projects no limit definitions, so this infrastructure does not invent usage or change existing operations. Each future metered workflow must declare its code-owned key and wrap its business effect with reservation and commit/release explicitly.

Lease lifecycle is `active -> refresh_due -> offline_lease -> grace -> restricted`; `revoked` and `invalid` override every other state. Restricted mode preserves sign-in, read access, backup/export, licensing repair, and explicitly documented safety-critical workflows. It blocks new high-risk writes, financial posting, destructive changes, and external Agent actions. Expiry or revocation never deletes, encrypts, or withholds a campus's own data.

Online installations refresh periodically. Offline installations may import a signed `.cp-license` bundle. A control-plane revocation takes effect on the next successful refresh or when the locally signed lease reaches its bounded validity deadline; the operational request path never depends on control-plane availability.

## 5. Module navigation

- The launcher is a quiet, searchable campus map: recent modules first, then grouped modules. It must not become a dense wall of identical cards.
- Each operational module owns its local navigation and provides a clear “All modules” return path.
- Administration navigation is limited to overview, users, roles and access, licensing, school settings, and the Agent management pages the signed-in administrator may open.
- Unimplemented licensed modules route to an honest setup/coming-soon state; there are no dead links or invented operational data.
- Secondary creation and editing flows use accessible right-side drawers. Centered modals are not used.

## 6. Security boundary

- The server is authoritative for both module enablement and permissions; hiding a launcher tile is never treated as authorization.
- The authenticated request context contains tenant ID, immutable role keys, effective permissions, and a freshly loaded entitlement snapshot. The snapshot distinguishes signed lease state, module state, projected feature grants, application-version compatibility, and future hard-limit exhaustion.
- Each API request receives a server-generated request ID and either propagates a valid incoming correlation ID or starts a new correlation. Successful authentication adds a person actor identity. Consequential domain transactions append reduced, redacted evidence to the actor-aware audit ledger using that context; request or correlation identifiers are never authorization inputs.
- Permission checks require both an enabled module and a matching permission. Campus Owner wildcard access still respects module enablement.
- License keys are never stored in plaintext or returned by the API.
- Support access to a campus, if added later, must be explicit, time-bound, and auditable.

## 7. Terminology

- Use “Sign in”, “All modules”, “Administration”, “Roles and access”, and “Licensing” consistently.
- “Campus Owner” is the campus-level super administrator. “Platform operator” is reserved for the future cross-campus platform console.
- Use “module” for a major product area and “permission” for an action inside a module.

## 8. Timetable lifecycle

- Timetabling follows `Rules and setup -> Generate draft -> Review -> Publish`, retains run history, and binds each new run to the active term.
- Attendance follows `Create class register -> Mark complete roster -> Submit -> Reasoned reopen when correction is required`. The register is a full-page workspace; creation, submission, reopen, and deletion confirmations use right-side drawers.
- E-learning follows `Create class space -> Add governed resources -> Publish resources -> Publish units -> Publish space -> Archive when complete`. Space and unit records use full pages; creation, editing, linking, upload, publication, withdrawal, archive, and configuration use right-side drawers.
- Gradebook follows `Open assessment cycle -> Create mark sheet -> Capture complete roster -> Submit -> Publish`, with a retained reason required to reopen submitted or published marks. Mark capture is a full-page workspace; creation, submission, publication, reopen, and deletion confirmations use right-side drawers.
- Progress & reporting follows `Configure grading scheme -> Generate from closed cycle -> Review remarks and progression -> Publish`, with a retained reason required to reopen reviewed or published batches. Report batches and transcripts are full pages; configuration, generation, remarks, progression, transitions, and confirmations use right-side drawers.
- Communication follows `Draft -> Review and freeze recipients -> Publish to inbox`, with a retained reason required to reopen a reviewed draft or cancel a publication. Announcement records and inbox messages use full-page workspaces; compose, edit, review, publication, reopen, cancellation, deletion, and message reading use right-side drawers.
- A generation run stores the exact configuration snapshot used for the result. Later setup edits never silently rewrite an existing draft or published timetable.
- Class, teacher, room, and teacher-unavailability collisions are hard constraints. Day balance and avoiding repeated same-subject days are quality preferences.
- Generation may return unresolved lessons, but a run with unresolved lessons cannot be published.
- Publication supersedes the prior published run for the same campus; it does not delete historical runs.
- Academics is authoritative for the active academic year and term, canonical grade levels, classes, subjects, employee-backed teachers, teaching assignments, assessment structures, Gradebook lifecycle, and published academic reporting. SIS applications and staged imports resolve grade references through Academics; Timetabling, Attendance, Gradebook, and Progress & reporting rehydrate records through typed owning-module operations and never accept client copies as authoritative. Assessment components resolve class, subject, and teacher through their teaching assignment. Attendance and Gradebook consume SIS enrolments through a typed owning-module boundary; reporting consumes their published/submitted evidence without rewriting it.
- Timetabling owns teaching days, periods, rooms, recurring teacher slot restrictions, per-assignment room preferences, generated snapshots, and publication state. Approved dated HR availability overlapping the active term is included as a minimum-field planning constraint in new snapshots; it is not converted into a recurring restriction. A teacher name change in HR is reflected in new configuration reads without rewriting an existing timetable snapshot.

## 9. Agent access

- Agent is a licensed module and follows the same login, role, permission, module-enablement, and record-scope rules as every other module.
- Agent has stable module key `agent`, route `/modules/agent`, and permission namespace `agent`.
- Agent capabilities never outrank the signed-in person. Effective access also applies campus Agent policy and any required human approval.
- Every server-owned product operation is classified for Agent as executable, approval-required, human-only, or prohibited. No operation may be left unclassified.
- Provider administration, routing, capability policy, limits, campus-wide usage, and run audit are Administration concerns with distinct permissions.
- Dynamic custom roles may receive Agent permissions and capability policy; seeded role names are not used as authorization shortcuts.
- New-campus School Administrator seeds receive the current AI-provider and routing Administration permissions. Migration 084 backfills only `ai_providers:view/edit` and `ai_routing:view/edit` for existing, non-deleted built-in School Administrator roles. It does not grant Agent-use permissions, module entitlements, leases, or assignments; all other existing non-owner roles remain unchanged.
- The canonical capability, provider, metering, and approval model is `docs/agent-platform.md`.
